# Problem 401: Sum of Squares of Divisors

The divisors of $6$ are $1,2,3$ and $6$.  
The sum of the squares of these numbers is $1+4+9+36=50$.

Let $\\mathrm{sigma}\_2(n)$ represent the sum of the squares of the divisors of $n$. Thus $\\mathrm{sigma}\_2(6)=50$.

Let $\\mathrm{SIGMA}\_2$ represent the summatory function of $\\mathrm{sigma}\_2$, that is $\\mathrm{SIGMA}\_2(n)=\\sum \\mathrm{sigma}\_2(i)$ for $i=1$ to $n$.  
The first $6$ values of $\\mathrm{SIGMA}\_2$ are: $1,6,16,37,63$ and $113$.

Find $\\mathrm{SIGMA}\_2(10^{15})$ modulo $10^9$.
